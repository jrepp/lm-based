# Sizing the 2026 open-weight frontier to 128GB

The most capable model you should run locally as of October 2026 is **Qwen3-Coder-Next (80B-A3B, Apache-2.0) at Q6_K** for your primary coding/agentic workload — it is the only model that fits a 128GB M5 Max with independently verified agentic-coding scores (70.6 SWE-bench Verified, 44.3 SWE-bench Pro, 36.2 Terminal-Bench 2.0), it is already sidecarred in this repo, and it delivers roughly **50–75 tok/s** with a 262K-token context costing only ~6GB of KV cache ([Qwen3-Coder-Next card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-Coder-Next)). Pair it with **Qwen3.5-122B-A10B at Q4_K_M (76.5GB)** as the most capable *general-use* model that genuinely fits — Apache-2.0, native vision, 262K native context — with the explicit caveat that its capability rests on vendor-reported numbers, not independent benchmarks ([unsloth GGUF](https://huggingface.co/unsloth/Qwen3.5-122B-A10B-GGUF)). The 2026 "flash" frontier that dominates headlines — Qwen3.8-Flash-Next, GLM-5.3-Flash, DeepSeek-V4-Flash, MiniMax-M2.5 — does **not** genuinely fit this machine: Flash-Next's architecture keeps a ~98GiB embedding hash table in host RAM on top of a ~94GB GPU footprint, and the others only fit at ≤3-bit quality. Keep your Qwen3.6-27B draft-MTP setup as the low-latency daily driver and upgrade it to **Qwen3.8-27B Q6_K (22GB)** once validated ([Qwen3.8-27B card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.8-27B)).

## Verified benchmarks leave Qwen3-Coder-Next unbeaten in the fits-in-128GB class

The open-weights frontier in late 2026 is real but out of reach: GLM-5 (744B MoE, MIT) self-reports **77.8 SWE-bench Verified, 56.2 Terminal-Bench 2.0, and 89.7 τ²-Bench** — within ~2 points of Claude Opus 4.5 on agentic tasks — but at 744B it needs 256GB-class hardware, and the same goes for Kimi K2.5 (76.8 SWE-V, ~1T) and DeepSeek-V3.2 (73.1) ([GLM-5 card](https://huggingface.co/zai-org/GLM-5)). These numbers matter mainly as a capability ceiling reference: Qwen3-Coder-Next gives up only **~7 SWE-bench Verified points** against the open leader while activating **~3B parameters instead of 40B**, which is precisely why it is fast enough to be pleasant on Apple Silicon. Its evals are HF-leaderboard-verified rather than self-graded, its card explicitly targets Claude Code/Cline/Qwen Code harnesses and local development, and its adoption is unmatched — 594K downloads/month and 115 community quantizations within months of release ([Qwen3-Coder-Next card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-Coder-Next)).

Two caveats carry through. First, the standout **SWE-bench Pro 44.3** score comes from Qwen's own tech report and has not been independently re-run — treat it as promising, not settled. Second, the leaderboard infrastructure lags the 2026 cycle: the Aider polyglot board has no entries for GLM-5, Qwen3-Coder-Next, or any Qwen3.5/3.8 model, and its best open score remains DeepSeek-V3.2-Exp at 74.2% — while **gpt-oss-120b sits at 41.8%**, disqualifying it as a capability pick despite fitting trivially at 63GB ([aider.chat](https://aider.chat/docs/leaderboards/)). One reconciliation across the research notes: the benchmark note found "no evidence" of open Qwen3.5/3.6/3.8 releases, but that reflected a narrow fetch set (leaderboards and vendor pages) — the landscape note verified Qwen3.5-122B-A10B, Qwen3.8-27B, and Qwen3.8-Flash-Next directly against the Hugging Face API, and your own repo already serves Qwen3.6-27B. The substantive gap survives, though: **no independent benchmark scores exist for Qwen3.5-122B, Qwen3.8, GLM-5.3-Flash, or MiniMax-M2.5** — only vendor cards, download counts, and positioning claims.

## The 96–120GB wired budget disqualifies the flash frontier

macOS wires ~75% of unified memory to the GPU by default (**96GB** on this machine), raisable via `sudo sysctl iogpu.wired_limit_mb=122880` to ~120GB, with ~8GB left for macOS; the sensible planning band is **96GB stock, 112–118GB tuned** — and since llama-swap can transiently co-host two models, prefer the conservative end ([stencel.io](https://stencel.io/posts/apple-silicon-limitations-with-usage-on-local-llm%20.html), [RunAIHome](https://runaihome.com/blog/best-local-llm-128gb-unified-memory-2026/)). Against that budget the notes disagree on exactly one model, and the disagreement decides the recommendation. The landscape note crowns **Qwen3.8-Flash-Next** (180B on disk, 6B active) the capability ceiling at UD-Q4_K_XL 111.3GB / IQ4_XS 93.7GB ([unsloth GGUF](https://huggingface.co/unsloth/Qwen3.8-Flash-Next-GGUF)). The platform note counters with the llama.cpp port's fine print: the `qwen4exp` architecture keeps its **~97.7GiB per-layer token-embedding (n-gram) hash table in host RAM**, outside the wired pool — so ~94GB GPU plus the host table exceeds 128GB *physical*, making it 256GB-machine class despite the attractive file size ([PR #27742](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/27742)). The platform analysis wins because it reflects how the model actually loads; add a custom `qwen-community-1.0` license and open issues (context-depth decode decay, tool-call leakage, concurrency crashes) ([issue #30307](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/issues/30307)), and Flash-Next lands on the watchlist, not the shortlist.

The rest of the flash tier fails on quantization quality. **GLM-5.3-Flash** (320B/A18B, MIT) needs ≤2.5-bit to squeeze under 120GB (UD-IQ2_XXS 101.8GB) — at that bit rate it is dominated by a 4-bit Qwen3.5-122B, though it becomes the top pick on 192GB+ hardware ([unsloth GGUF](https://huggingface.co/unsloth/GLM-5.3-Flash-GGUF)). **MiniMax-M2.5** fits only at UD-Q3_K_XL 101.3GB and **DeepSeek-V4-Flash** only at UD-IQ3_S 116.1GB — the latter leaves essentially zero KV headroom even on the raised limit, killing the 1M context that is its main selling point ([unsloth MiniMax](https://huggingface.co/unsloth/MiniMax-M2.5-GGUF), [unsloth DeepSeek](https://huggingface.co/unsloth/DeepSeek-V4-Flash-0731-GGUF)). What remains is a clean fits-at-≥Q4 tier: Qwen3.5-122B (60–101GB), Qwen3-Coder-Next (48–85GB), Qwen3.8-27B (17–29GB), Qwen3.5-35B-A3B (~22GB est.), gpt-oss-120b (63GB), and Mistral-Small-4-119B (~73–97GB est., but dense and therefore slow). Everything at 355B and above — GLM-4.6/4.7/5.x, Kimi K2.x/K3, DeepSeek V3.2/V4-Pro/V4.1, Qwen3.5-397B, Qwen3.8-2.4T, MiniMax-M3, Mistral-Large-3 — is out at any usable quant.

## Throughput and KV math on the M5 Max set the serving config

The M5 Max's 614 GB/s bandwidth buys only ~12% more decode than M4 Max, but its per-core Neural Accelerators deliver up to 4× prompt-processing gains — gains currently realized in MLX, with llama.cpp Metal support undocumented ([LLMCheck](https://llmcheck.net/blog/apple-silicon-m5-max-local-ai-guide/)). The only first-party M5 Max 128GB measurements are MLX-based: **Qwen3.5-122B-A10B 4-bit at 65.9 tok/s @4K falling to 54.9 @32K; Qwen3-Coder-Next 8-bit at 79.3 @4K falling to 48.2 @64K; gpt-oss-120b Q8 at 87.9 → 64.5 @32K** ([Hardware Corner](https://www.hardware-corner.net/m5-max-local-llm-benchmarks-20261233/)). Since llama.cpp historically decodes ~10–15% below MLX for the same model, plan on **~47–59 tok/s for Qwen3.5-122B Q4_K_M, ~50–75 tok/s for Coder-Next Q6_K/Q8 at shallow context**, and ~20–24 tok/s for the 27B dense model — roughly **doubled to ~35–45 effective tok/s by your existing draft-MTP setup** (llama.cpp draft-MTP merged May 2026 reports 72–83% acceptance and >2× speedup; DFlash2 measured 1.85× on an M5 Pro) ([PR #22673](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/22673), [PR #27342](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/27342)). Note one earlier note's claim that Mac-side MTP is "mostly a datacenter story" is outdated — the merged PR and your own daily-driver config refute it.

Long-context memory is the other budget line, and it strongly favors the hybrid-attention architectures you already serve. KV quantization to q8_0 is near-lossless (1.88× compression, PPL 13.8348 vs 13.8387 f16) and q4_0 acceptable (3.56×) ([PR #21089](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/21089)); the `qwen3next` hybrid allocates just **6,144 MiB of KV at the full 262,144-token context (~2.3GiB/100K at f16)**, so the 1M YaRN profile in `lm_launcher/profiles.py` costs ~23GB f16 or ~12GB at q8_0 — comfortably servable from a 65GB Q6_K file on the default 96GB limit ([issue #27727](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/issues/27727)). Standard-attention models pay far more: expect ~10–30GB+ for very long contexts on Qwen3.5-122B, so serve it at **131–262K context with q8_0 KV on the raised wired limit**, not at its headline 1M extension. Operationally, pin llama-server at a v0.6.x tag (a post-v0.6.0 regression aborts qwen4exp+MTP on image input, and your Qwen3.6 MTP setup shares the PR #22673 path), and validate first-token coherence on Metal for qwen3next before trusting it — all its filed bugs are ROCm/CUDA, and absence of Metal reports is not proof of correctness.

## Conclusion

The interesting shift in 2026 is that the 128GB ceiling is no longer defined by file size but by architectural memory tricks: Qwen3.8-Flash-Next's n-gram embedding table and DeepSeek's 1M-context ambition both trade RAM capacity in ways that make GGUF-gigabyte math lie. The reliable winners on this machine are the models whose capability *and* memory behavior are documented — Qwen3-Coder-Next above all, which sits 7 SWE-bench Verified points from the open-weight leader while running at one-thirteenth the active parameters. That gap is small enough that the practical question flips: rather than hunting for a bigger model, use the repo's own harness as the benchmark — serve Qwen3.5-122B-A10B, measure it against Coder-Next on your actual agentic tasks, and let observed tok/s and task success, not vendor tables, settle which flagship-class claims survive contact with 614 GB/s.
