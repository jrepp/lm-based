# Small parsers dethrone frontier VLMs on PDFs

The best small open-weight PDF/document parser for this stack as of October 2026 is **GLM-OCR (0.9B, MIT)** — **95.22 on OmniDocBench v1.6_full**, with an official ggml-org GGUF, a 4-bit MLX build, and merged llama.cpp support — with **PaddleOCR-VL-1.6 (0.9B, Apache-2.0, 96.34 v1.6, best formula CDM 97.53)** as the statistical near-tie and **Chandra-OCR-2 (5.3B, 85.8/85.9 olmOCR-Bench)** as the quality-max alternative to load on demand. Select against **two benchmarks simultaneously**: olmOCR-Bench, whose pass/fail unit tests best predict real-world English PDF→markdown quality, and OmniDocBench v1.6_full, which scores structural fidelity (table TEDS, formula CDM, reading order) and functions as the de facto live leaderboard ([OmniDocBench GitHub](https://github.com/opendatalab/OmniDocBench), [olmOCR-Bench README](https://github.com/allenai/olmocr/blob/main/olmocr/bench/README.md)). The striking 2026 result is that sub-1B specialized parsers now beat frontier VLMs outright — PaddleOCR-VL-1.6's 96.34 exceeds Gemini 3 Pro's 92.91 — so the general-purpose Qwen3-VL models in your slate should not be used as parsers (Qwen3-VL-8B manages only 64.6 on olmOCR-Bench) ([OmniDocBench GitHub](https://github.com/opendatalab/OmniDocBench), [Chandra GitHub](https://github.com/datalab-to/chandra)). Memory is a non-issue: every candidate fits in ≤4GB against your ~120GB usable budget alongside a 77GB flagship, and throughput — output-token-bound at ~2,400 tokens per dense page — is the real constraint ([Surya card](https://huggingface.co/datalab-to/surya-ocr-2-gguf)). The launcher's missing mmproj wiring is a one-time generic fix in `lm_launcher/profiles.py`, and the official ggml-org/PaddlePaddle/datalab GGUFs are the easy path; DeepSeek-OCR-2, Baidu Unlimited-OCR, and dots.mocr pin custom NVIDIA-centric runtimes with no usable GGUF, so they stay off the shortlist ([llama.cpp multimodal.md](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/docs/multimodal.md), [DeepSeek-OCR-2 card](https://huggingface.co/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-OCR-2)).

## Select against olmOCR-Bench for real-world markdown and OmniDocBench v1.6 for structure

OmniDocBench (opendatalab, CVPR 2025) is the structural benchmark: 1,651 PDF pages spanning 10 document types, bilingual CN/EN, scored as **Overall = ((1 − text edit distance)×100 + table TEDS + formula CDM) / 3**, with reading-order edit distance and layout mAP reported separately ([OmniDocBench GitHub](https://github.com/opendatalab/OmniDocBench)). Version discipline matters: v1.6 (Apr 2026) added MGAM matching and 296 hard pages (nested tables, dense formulas, unconventional layouts), re-baselining all scores, so v1.0/v1.5/v1.6 numbers are not comparable; the current board is **v1.6_full** in the GitHub README, last updated 2026/09/11, and an EvalScope integration added 2026/07/27 runs the benchmark directly against OpenAI-compatible endpoints — meaning you can evaluate candidates against your own llama-swap URLs rather than trusting vendor tables ([OmniDocBench GitHub](https://github.com/opendatalab/OmniDocBench)). olmOCR-Bench (Ai2) is the real-world proxy: **~7,000 machine-checkable pass/fail test cases over ~1,400 English single-page PDFs** across 8 categories (ArXiv math, old-scans math, tables, old scans, headers/footers, multi-column, long tiny text, base), deliberately rejecting edit distance because a correct parse may legitimately differ from the reference ([olmOCR-Bench README](https://github.com/allenai/olmocr/blob/main/olmocr/bench/README.md)). For "will this parser produce usable markdown from messy English PDFs," **trust olmOCR-Bench first** — its design targets exactly the failure modes that ruin downstream LLM ingestion, Datalab calls it "the widely accepted" benchmark and runs it on rivals, and 2026 vendor write-ups (docsumo, Roboflow) pair the two boards as the standard selection method ([Chandra GitHub](https://github.com/datalab-to/chandra), [Docsumo](https://www.docsumo.com/blog/best-ocr-models)). OmniDocBench then contributes what olmOCR-Bench cannot: per-component table/formula scores, reading order, and Chinese coverage. The live leaderboards are the OmniDocBench GitHub README (the de facto live board), the olmOCR repo results table (which marks self-reported rows with `*`), the OCRBench v2 GitHub-pages board (quarterly, last 2026.03 — but it is a VLM QA benchmark, not a parsing benchmark), and the MDPBench HF Space; legacy DocVQA/ChartQA/TextVQA sets measure field-level QA, not page reconstruction, and no 2026 source treats them as decisive ([MultimodalOCR GitHub](https://github.com/Yuliang-Liu/MultimodalOCR)). Carry MDPBench's warning throughout: open parsers drop **~17.8% on photographed pages**, so all clean-scan scores overstate real-world robustness ([MultimodalOCR GitHub](https://github.com/Yuliang-Liu/MultimodalOCR)).

## The leaderboard conflicts are complementary signals, not contradictions

The headline conflict — Chandra-OCR-2 tops olmOCR-Bench while TeleOCR/GLM-OCR/PaddleOCR-VL top OmniDocBench — dissolves once you note the boards measure different things. On olmOCR-Bench the open-weights leader is **Chandra-OCR-2 at 85.8 (Datalab-run) / 85.9 (self-reported)**, ahead of dots.ocr 1.5 (83.9, self-reported), **Surya-OCR-2 (83.3, "best under 3B params")**, and olmOCR-2-7B (82.4, Ai2-run) ([Chandra GitHub](https://github.com/datalab-to/chandra), [Surya card](https://huggingface.co/datalab-to/surya-ocr-2-gguf)). On OmniDocBench v1.6_full the top tier is all small specialized models — **TeleOCR (1.2B) 96.91, OvisOCR2 (0.8B) 96.47, PaddleOCR-VL-1.6 (0.9B) 96.34**, MinerU2.5-Pro (1.2B) 95.75, GLM-OCR (0.9B) 95.22 — with every frontier model below them: Gemini 3 Pro 92.91, Qwen3-VL-235B 89.78, GPT-5.2 86.59 ([OmniDocBench GitHub](https://github.com/opendatalab/OmniDocBench)). One reconciliation matters for the notes: **GLM-OCR's widely quoted "#1 at 94.62" is a v1.5-era claim** — the v1.6 MGAM re-baseline plus the 2026/09/11 leaderboard additions (TeleOCR, OvisOCR2, Unlimited-OCR) pushed it to fifth, which is why the model card and the current board disagree ([GLM-OCR card](https://huggingface.co/zai-org/GLM-OCR), [OmniDocBench GitHub](https://github.com/opendatalab/OmniDocBench)). Cross-benchmark disagreement is itself informative rather than contradictory: olmOCR-2 leads headers/footers (96.1) yet sits at just 85.74 on OmniDocBench; dots.ocr is mid-pack on OmniDocBench (90.77) but strong on olmOCR-Bench (83.9); Nanonets-OCR2-3B looks acceptable on the former and poor on the latter (69.5 overall, a catastrophic 32.1 on headers/footers) ([Chandra GitHub](https://github.com/datalab-to/chandra), [OmniDocBench GitHub](https://github.com/opendatalab/OmniDocBench)). Because OmniDocBench is CN/EN and its top ranks are Chinese-lab models computed from author-supplied inference scripts, an English-first workload should lean on olmOCR-Bench category scores and treat OmniDocBench ordering as roughly 1–2 points soft. One factual discrepancy between the note sets: Datalab's README calls Chandra 2 a 4B model while the HF model card verified this session says **5.3B** — use the card figure ([chandra-ocr-2 card](https://huggingface.co/datalab-to/chandra-ocr-2)).

The memory math is trivial on this machine: a 0.9B VLM is roughly 0.6–0.8GB of weights at Q4 plus mmproj and KV cache, and even Chandra-OCR-2 lands around 3–4GB — noise against the ~120GB usable budget already planning for a 76.5GB flagship, and llama-swap can keep a parser permanently resident (estimates from param counts, not measured files). Throughput is the actual constraint: page parsing is output-token-bound at ~2,360 tokens per page, and the only published Apple Silicon figures — Surya-OCR-2 via llama.cpp/Metal — show **0.108 pages/s ≈ 254 tok/s at ~30W with `--parallel 8`, p50 59s per dense page**, with vendor claims of 1.86 pages/s for GLM-OCR on unspecified hardware ([Surya card](https://huggingface.co/datalab-to/surya-ocr-2-gguf), [GLM-OCR card](https://huggingface.co/zai-org/GLM-OCR)). Expect the other sub-1B parsers to land in the same single-stream band and scale with batching; nobody publishes Metal numbers for GLM-OCR or PaddleOCR-VL, so measure rather than assume. The universal llama.cpp caveat is that **OCR models are trained with specific prompt and input structures** and some need large contexts (`-c 8192`), so each profile must carry the model's documented prompt template ([llama.cpp multimodal.md](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/docs/multimodal.md)).

On the launcher's missing mmproj wiring, the per-candidate work differs sharply. The generic fix — a `mmproj` field in the sidecar schema plus `--mmproj` emission in `lm_launcher/profiles.py` — is a one-time change measured in hours; after that, **GLM-OCR and PaddleOCR-VL-1.6 are trivial** because the official ggml-org/PaddlePaddle GGUFs are built for llama.cpp with merged multimodal PRs, and olmOCR-2 rides the mature `qwen2_5_vl` path. **Surya-OCR-2 is easy for the VLM but medium for the pipeline**: Datalab ships the official GGUF and can point `SURYA_INFERENCE_URL` at your own running server, but layout detection runs in a separate small torch model outside llama.cpp ([Surya card](https://huggingface.co/datalab-to/surya-ocr-2-gguf)). **Chandra-OCR-2 is the uncertain one** — only a community GGUF exists with unverified quality and mmproj, and the recommended serving path is vLLM ([chandra-ocr-2 card](https://huggingface.co/datalab-to/chandra-ocr-2)).

## Recommendation: GLM-OCR as default, Chandra-OCR-2 for quality, skip the custom-runtime lineage

**Primary pick: GLM-OCR** as the always-resident llama-swap parser. It combines a permissive MIT license, the official ggml-org GGUF (the easiest mmproj path for the launcher), an MLX 4-bit build, merged llama.cpp support, 1.70M downloads (second only to DeepSeek-OCR's 2.04M among dedicated parsers), and a top-tier 95.22 on the current OmniDocBench ([GLM-OCR card](https://huggingface.co/zai-org/GLM-OCR), [HF API](https://huggingface.co/api/models?search=ocr)). Its known gap is the absence of a published olmOCR-Bench row in the tables retrieved, so its real-world English markdown quality is inferred rather than measured — if formula/table-heavy or Chinese documents dominate, switch the default to **PaddleOCR-VL-1.6**, which posts the higher v1.6 score (96.34) with the best formula CDM (97.53) and equally complete packaging. **Quality-max alternative: Chandra-OCR-2 on demand** — at 85.8 it sits within one point of Datalab's own hosted API tier (86.7), but it carries three caveats: modified **OpenRAIL-M weights** (free for research, personal use, and startups under $2M funding/revenue, and cannot be used competitively with Datalab's API — fine for a home lab, not for anything commercial), a community-only GGUF whose quantization quality is unverified, and no MLX port ([chandra-ocr-2 card](https://huggingface.co/datalab-to/chandra-ocr-2)). If you want maximum quality under a clean license instead, olmOCR-2-7B (82.4, Ai2-reproduced, best-in-class headers/footers at 96.1, mature GGUF + MLX) is the fallback ([Chandra GitHub](https://github.com/datalab-to/chandra)).

**Skip, with reasons:** DeepSeek-OCR-2 (90.25 on v1.6 but pins `transformers==4.46.3` + `flash-attn==2.7.3` on CUDA with no GGUF found), Baidu Unlimited-OCR (requires a custom no-repeat-ngram logit processor even on vLLM/SGLang; its community GGUF's llama.cpp claim is unverified), dots.mocr (83.9 olmOCR-Bench but no GGUF or llama.cpp path), Qwen3-VL-8B as a parser (64.6 — keep it for general vision, not parsing), Nanonets-OCR2-3B (69.5), GOT-OCR2.0 (48.3), and Granite-Docling-258M (pipeline-style, no GGUF found) ([DeepSeek-OCR-2 card](https://huggingface.co/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-OCR-2), [Unlimited-OCR card](https://huggingface.co/baidu/Unlimited-OCR), [Chandra GitHub](https://github.com/datalab-to/chandra)). **Watchlist:** TeleOCR (96.91) and OvisOCR2 (96.47) top the v1.6 board but their GGUF/MLX availability is unverified; MinerU2.5-Flash-0.8B (days old) and LightOnOCR-2-1B (83.2, methodology caveat) show the frontier still moving smaller ([OmniDocBench GitHub](https://github.com/opendatalab/OmniDocBench), [Surya card](https://huggingface.co/datalab-to/surya-ocr-2-gguf)). The standing caveats: the olmOCR-Bench star system mixes self-reported and reproduced rows, OmniDocBench rows are computed from author-supplied scripts on a CN/EN benchmark, vendor throughput numbers are self-reported, no r/LocalLLaMA threads could be fetched (HTTP 403 — "community consensus" here is triangulated from HF adoption, leaderboards, and first-class llama.cpp support, not forum sentiment), and absent MLX ports are absence of evidence rather than proof of impossibility.

## Conclusion

The 2026 parser market has inverted the usual local-model calculus: capability-per-gigabyte no longer matters because a state-of-the-art parser costs less than 4GB, so selection reduces to benchmark-fit, serving path, and license — three axes where the notes disagree in recoverable ways. The benchmark conflict is the most useful signal: olmOCR-Bench's pass/fail unit tests and OmniDocBench's component scores reward genuinely different behaviors, and a model leading one board tells you what it optimizes for, not that the other board is wrong. Since OmniDocBench's EvalScope integration evaluates any OpenAI-compatible endpoint, the repo can make every vendor number falsifiable on its own hardware — run it against your llama-swap URLs before trusting any card claim, including these. The durable shift is that licenses became the real differentiator at the quality frontier: the OpenRAIL-capped Datalab models own the top of olmOCR-Bench while MIT/Apache models own OmniDocBench's top tier, so "best" now depends on whether the deployment is a home lab or a product.
